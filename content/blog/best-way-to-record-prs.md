A PR happens once. You don't get a second take on the first time 315 leaves the floor, which means the recording setup deserves the same seriousness as the warm-up. Here's how to run PR day so the attempt is captured well, reviewed properly, and celebrated the way it deserves.

## Before the attempt: set up like it matters

- **Set the camera during warm-ups, not before the max.** Frame your last warm-up set exactly as you'll frame the attempt, watch it back once, and adjust. When the big set comes, you just hit record — no fiddling while adrenaline builds.
- **Use the right angle for the lift.** Side-on at knee height for deadlifts, 45-degree front at hip height for squats, foot-end angle just above pad height for bench. (Full breakdown in [our form-check filming guide](/blog/how-to-film-your-lifts-for-form-checks).) A PR filmed from a useless angle is a memory, not a record.
- **Frame wide enough for the whole lift.** Feet to bar overhead for Olympic lifts, full plate diameter for pulls. Cutting off the lockout is the classic PR-day heartbreak.
- **Lock the tripod down.** PR attempts attract dropped bars and stomping feet. A phone leaning on a chalk bucket will not survive the celebration.
- **Start recording early.** Hit record before your setup ritual, not as you approach the bar. Ten extra seconds of footage costs nothing; a clipped first pull costs everything.

## After the attempt: the honest review

Watch it once to celebrate. Then watch it again to learn — a max attempt is the most information-dense footage you'll capture all year, because maximal weight exposes what submaximal weight hides.

- **Bar path.** Scrub frame by frame. Did the bar drift forward off the floor? Loop on the squat? The deviation you see at 100 percent is the weak point to train at 80 percent.
- **Where the grind happened.** The slowest inches of the lift are your sticking point, on video, with no guesswork. That's next block's programming handed to you.
- **The legitimacy check.** Depth, lockout, full control. Be the strictest judge in the room — a PR list you can't fully trust is worse than a shorter honest one.
- **Setup consistency.** Compare against your previous PR clip. The lifters who progress for years have setups you could overlay frame for frame.

When someone asks how your deadlift has moved over two years, you don't guess; you filter to deadlift PRs and watch the whole story.

## The victory lap: celebrate with people who get it

A PR shared with nobody feels strangely incomplete, and a PR shared with ten thousand strangers feels strangely hollow. The right audience is the people who knew what the old max was.

That's your crew — the friends who spotted the failed attempt three weeks ago and know exactly what this number means. Share the clip with them, let them lose their minds appropriately, and return the energy when their PR clip shows up in your feed. In GMRL your PR goes out to your mutually-added gym friends as a story — no public feed, no strangers, just the people whose hype actually lands. That loop, honestly, is half of why PR day feels like a holiday.

Then add the clip to a highlights collection — a running "PRs" reel becomes the single most rewatchable thing in your library, and the best possible thing to watch during the warm-up on your next max day.

## FAQ

### What's the best camera setup for recording a PR attempt?

Frame and test the shot during warm-up sets, use the lift-appropriate angle (side-on at knee height for deadlifts, 45-degree front at hip height for squats), lock the phone on a tripod, and start recording before your setup ritual so nothing gets clipped.

### What should I look for when reviewing PR footage?

Check bar path frame by frame for drift or looping, find the slowest portion of the rep — that's your sticking point — and verify legitimacy: depth, lockout, and control. Max-effort footage reveals weaknesses that lighter training hides, so it directly informs your next program.

### How do I keep track of all my PRs?

Tag every PR clip at the point of capture with the exercise and a PR flag. In GMRL, PR-tagged clips form a filterable, dated, video-backed record — so your PR history is a list you can watch, not numbers you half-remember.

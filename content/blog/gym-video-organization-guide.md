Open your camera roll and count how many gym clips are in there. If you've been filming your training for a year, the answer is probably "hundreds," and they're scattered between screenshots, receipts, and photos of your dog. You know there's a great squat clip from March in there somewhere. You will never find it.

This is the quiet failure mode of filming your lifts: capture is easy, retrieval is impossible. Here's a system that fixes it.

## Why the camera roll fails

The camera roll organizes by one dimension only — time — and mixes your training footage with everything else in your life. That creates three problems:

- **No context.** A clip from April 12th doesn't tell you it was week 3 of your meet prep, or that it was a PR.
- **No filtering.** You can't ask your camera roll for "all deadlift clips" or "only PRs."
- **No lifespan.** When storage fills up, gym clips are the first thing people mass-delete — and the history goes with them.

Albums help a little, but manually sorting clips into albums after every session is a chore nobody sustains past a few weeks.

## The three-layer system

Good organization for training footage has three layers, in order of importance.

### Layer 1: Sessions, not files

The natural unit of training footage is the gym session, not the individual clip. "Tuesday's leg day" should be one group containing its four clips — because that's how you remember training and how you'll look for it later. This is the core of how GMRL structures your library: import clips and they group themselves into dated sessions automatically, so a month of training reads like a timeline of gym days instead of a wall of thumbnails.

### Layer 2: Exercise and PR tags

Within sessions, tag every clip with its exercise. Squat, bench, deadlift, plus custom tags for whatever you actually train — sled pushes, ring muscle-ups, pause squats. Then flag PRs separately. Two disciplined tags per clip is all it takes to make any question answerable in seconds: every bench clip this year, every PR ever, every pause-squat session since June.

> Rule of thumb: tag the clip the same day you film it. A clip untagged for a week stays untagged forever.

### Layer 3: Highlight reels

Sessions and tags are for finding footage; highlights are for enjoying it. Build small curated collections — "2026 PRs," "Squat progress," "Meet day" — from your best clips. A highlight reel is the difference between footage you store and footage you rewatch, and rewatching your own progress is genuinely useful motivation on the days training feels stuck.

## The one-time cleanup

If you've got a year of backlog, don't try to tag everything. Do this instead:

- Skim your camera roll and pull only the clips that matter: PRs, technique milestones, memorable sessions. Expect to keep maybe 20 percent.
- Import those into your library so they land in dated sessions.
- Tag the PRs first — they're the spine of your history — then exercise-tag the rest in one sitting with a coffee.
- Going forward, import and tag on the same day you film. Two minutes after each session keeps the system alive.

## What this unlocks

Once the library exists, things that were impossible become trivial: month-over-month form comparisons, an honest all-time PR list with video proof, a year-end highlight reel, and a form-check archive you can send a coach without twenty minutes of scrolling. And because clips live in an organized library instead of loose in the camera roll, you stop deleting your training history every time your phone runs out of space.

Your training deserves better than being buried between screenshots. Build the library once; it pays you back every month you train.

## FAQ

### What's the best way to organize workout videos on iPhone?

Group clips by gym session date, then tag each clip by exercise and flag PRs. Camera-roll albums can approximate this but require constant manual sorting; a dedicated app like GMRL creates dated sessions automatically on import and makes tags filterable, which keeps the system sustainable.

### Should I delete old gym videos to save storage?

Don't mass-delete your history — curate it. Keep PRs, technique milestones, and one or two top sets per week; delete redundant warm-up angles and failed takes. If storage is tight, an optional cloud backup preserves your PR archive without filling your phone.

### How do I make a highlight reel of my lifts?

Pick a theme — yearly PRs, one lift's progress, meet day — and collect your best 10 to 20 clips into a dedicated collection. In GMRL, highlights are built straight from tagged clips, so a "2026 PRs" reel takes minutes and updates every time you hit a new one.
